For a lot of Indian businesses, WhatsApp is the shop counter. Enquiries, orders, payment screenshots, complaints and follow-ups all arrive in the same place. That works until there are more chats than one phone can handle. Then leads go cold, two people reply to the same customer, and nobody knows who promised what.

You do not need a new tool. You need a little structure around the one your customers already use.

## Start with templates

Write down the ten messages you send most often. Price list, address and timings, payment details, order confirmation, "we have received your request". Save them as quick replies on the WhatsApp Business app, or as approved templates if you are on the Business API.

Templates do two things. Replies get faster, and they get consistent, so a customer gets the same answer whoever is on shift. Keep them short and in the language your customers write to you in. If most of them write in Hinglish, a formal English template reads as a bot.

## Make it easy to start a chat

Every place a customer meets you should open a chat in one tap. Your website, your Instagram bio, your Google Business profile, your ads, the QR code on your counter. A click-to-chat link can carry a prefilled message, such as "Hi, I want to know about the weekend batch". That tells you where the lead came from before you have typed a word.

## One inbox, not five phones

The moment more than one person handles chats, a personal phone gets hard to manage. Move to the Business API with a shared inbox. Everyone sees the same conversations, chats can be assigned to a person, and there is a record when someone leaves or goes on leave.

Agree three rules with your team. Who picks up new chats. How fast a first reply has to go out. When a chat counts as closed. Write them down. Most WhatsApp chaos is a missing rule, not a missing feature.

## When to automate

Automate the parts that are the same every time:

- the first reply outside business hours, with your timings and a promise of when you will answer
- collecting the basics from a new lead, such as name, city and what they need
- a follow-up when a lead goes quiet
- order and appointment confirmations and reminders
- broadcasts to customers who opted in to hear from you

Done by hand, these are where the day goes. Nobody misses the human touch in a reminder.

## When not to

Keep a person on anything that needs judgement. Complaints, refunds, custom quotes, a customer who is upset, a large order. A bot that loops an angry customer through a menu costs you more than the time it saved.

Every automated flow should have an exit. If the customer types something the flow does not understand, or asks for a person, hand the chat to a human and tell them someone will reply.

Also, do not broadcast to people who did not ask. Meta tracks blocks and reports, a low quality rating limits how many people you can message, and customers mute businesses that spam.

## The order to do it in

Templates first, because they cost nothing. Click-to-chat links next. Then the shared inbox, once a second person starts replying. Automation last, once you know which messages repeat.
