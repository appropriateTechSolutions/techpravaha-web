Most studios hide their prices. You fill a form, you get a call, you describe your business to someone who is mostly trying to work out your budget. A week later a quote arrives. It is either fine, or so far off that both of you have wasted the week.

We put our prices on the [pricing page](/pricing) instead. Every packaged service has a number next to it, ex-GST, with the scope written underneath. Here is why.

## You can decide before you talk to anyone

A founder looking for a landing page usually knows three things: what they need, roughly when, and what they can spend. A visible price answers the third question in five seconds. If it fits, you read the scope. If it does not, you leave without being chased for a month.

That is a better outcome for you, and it is a better outcome for us. The people who do get in touch already know what it costs. The first conversation is about the work, not about money.

## A fixed price needs a fixed scope

You cannot print a price for "a website". You can print a price for one landing page, up to six sections, mobile-first, with a contact form, a WhatsApp button and search basics, on your domain, with a handover walkthrough.

Writing that list is the hard part. It forces us to decide what the package is, what it is not, and where the edges sit. Every line in a tier is work we only package when we can predict how long it takes.

This is also what makes the price honest. If a fixed scope turns out bigger than we thought, we carry that risk; you do not get a surprise bill.

## What it changes for us

Packaged work means repeatable work. We reuse the same build setup, the same checklists and the same handover document. We get faster at it, and that is what lets us print a price and a timeline.

It also changes how we say no. If a request falls outside the package, we say so in writing and quote it separately before doing it. No surprise invoice at the end.

## Where bespoke still applies

Some work does not fit a box. A site in three languages, an app that talks to your ERP, an assistant that takes bookings on its own. Each service has a Bespoke tier for exactly this.

Bespoke does not mean open-ended. We scope it with you first, then agree a fixed price, per phase if the project is large. You still know the number before we start. What you lose is the instant answer, because the number depends on your systems and we have to look at them.

If your project is close to a package but not quite, tell us. We will say plainly whether it fits a package or needs a Bespoke quote.

## What we ask in return

A clear brief and quick feedback. Fixed prices work when both sides move. We send work in short cycles, and the scope holds as long as the replies keep coming.

You see the price and what it covers. The kickoff date is fixed when you book, and the clock starts there. If that sounds like how you want to buy, start with the [pricing page](/pricing).
