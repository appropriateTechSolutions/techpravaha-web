A landing page has one job: get the right visitor to take one step. Everything on it either helps with that step or gets in the way. Here is what we put on every landing page we build, and what we leave off.

## The six things that matter

### 1. One promise

The first screen says what you do, for whom, and what changes for them. One sentence, in their words, not yours. "Accounting software for small clinics" beats "Empowering healthcare through innovation". If a visitor cannot repeat your promise after five seconds, the rest of the page is working uphill.

### 2. Proof

A claim with nothing behind it reads as noise. Proof is whatever a sceptical buyer would accept: real client names with their permission, screenshots of the product, a short quote with a name attached, the number of years you have been doing this. Use what you have. Do not invent what you do not.

### 3. One action

Pick the single thing you want a visitor to do. Book a call, message on WhatsApp, start a trial, buy. Repeat that one button down the page. Two competing buttons in the hero split attention, and three is a menu.

### 4. Speed

Most visitors to an Indian business site arrive on a phone, often on a patchy connection. If the page is slow, they leave before they see the promise. Compress images, ship less JavaScript, and test on a mid-range Android phone, not your laptop on office Wi-Fi. Speed is not a technical nicety. It decides whether anyone reads your copy at all.

### 5. Search basics

A page title and meta description written for a person searching, one clear heading, descriptive image alt text, a sitemap, and the page submitted for indexing. None of this is clever. Skipping it means paying for every visit.

### 6. A way to talk

Some visitors are ready but have one question. Give them a WhatsApp button or a short form, and answer the same day. In India, a WhatsApp link often does more than a contact form, because it is where your customers already are.

## What it doesn't need

**A slider.** Few people wait for slide three. Pick your strongest message and put it first.

**A wall of features.** List the three that matter to this visitor. The full list belongs on a product page.

**Stock photos of handshakes.** They signal that you had nothing real to show. A plain screenshot of your product is more convincing.

**Animations that delay the content.** A small fade is fine. A loader before the headline is not.

**Your company history.** Founding story, mission and values go on the About page. A landing page visitor has not decided to care yet.

**Every social icon.** Link the one channel you actually keep up, or none.

**A chat widget and a popup and a cookie banner all at once.** Each one covers part of the page the visitor came to read.

## How to check your own page

Open it on your phone. Read only the first screen. Ask whether a stranger would know what you do, whether they would believe you, and what they would tap next. If any answer is unclear, fix that before you add anything else.
